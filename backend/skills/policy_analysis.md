# Skill: Evidence-Aware Public Wellbeing Policy Analysis
1. Identify population, setting, policy objective and decision-maker.
2. Retrieve evidence with `search_policy_evidence` before making evidence claims.
3. Separate source-supported statements from policy inference and unknowns.
4. Compare practical options: universal or targeted programmes, organisational change, and access to qualified support.
5. Explain implementation requirements, benefits and trade-offs.
6. Consider staff time, costs, privacy, equity, stigma, cultural acceptability and opportunity cost.
7. Include safeguarding and referral pathways. Mindfulness is voluntary and does not replace clinical assessment or treatment.
8. Suggest metrics: reach, participation, accessibility, acceptability, validated wellbeing/stress measures, referral uptake, adverse experiences and equity.
9. Never invent statistics, effect sizes, costs or citations.
10. State evidence gaps and recommend a monitored pilot.

## Output headings
Executive recommendation; options and rationale; evidence and links; implementation; benefits and trade-offs; risks and equity; evaluation metrics; limitations.

## Evidence quality
A guideline or self-help guide is not proof that every local implementation will work. Match evidence to population and intervention. Avoid causal claims stronger than the source.
